# Liftoff product review — October 2026

A product-manager pass over liftoff: what it is, who it's for, what's wrong with it today, and the order we fix things in. Written against `main` at `860d3c8`, with two PRs in flight (the Simple view and company accounts).

## 1. What liftoff is

**One sentence:** liftoff is the provisioning control plane a B2B SaaS company plugs into its signup flow, so every new customer gets an isolated database, an address, API keys, a trial subscription and a welcome, in seconds, with a dashboard the company's support team can read.

**Who signs up:** a SaaS company (the *organisation*).
**Who the tenants are:** that company's customers.
**Who uses the dashboard:** the company's support and customer-success staff (the Simple view), and its engineers when something breaks (Under the hood).

That's the decision made on 2026-10-17. Before it, the app made the signer both the customer and the operator, which is why it felt like "a lot of moving parts".

## 2. What's wrong today

Ordered by how much they'd hurt a real company using it.

### Trust and correctness

| # | Problem | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Billing reports a Stripe subscription that doesn't exist.** With no Stripe key the step is *simulated*: the dashboard and the event log say "Stripe subscription active". | A support person would tell a customer their trial started. It didn't. Fake success is worse than an honest skip. | A `SKIPPED` step status. Billing is skipped unless the organisation has connected its own Stripe. Copy: "Billing: not connected". |
| T2 | **Billing uses liftoff's Stripe account**, not the company's. | A company's customers would be subscribed to *liftoff's* Stripe. Nobody can ship that. | Per-organisation Stripe credentials (encrypted at rest) and price IDs; later, Stripe Connect. Until connected, see T1. |
| T3 | **The welcome email is a mock, addressed to nobody.** | The customer never hears anything; the company doesn't either. | Notification step emails the *company* ("Globex is ready") via Resend, and the ready webhook carries the same payload. The customer-facing welcome is the company's job, triggered by the webhook. |
| T4 | **Cancel only works while provisioning, and nothing is undone.** An `ACTIVE` tenant can't be cancelled or deleted. Cancelling never drops the schema, revokes keys or cancels the subscription. | Resource leak on our side, and a data-retention problem for the company (a churned customer's data sits in our database forever). | A deprovisioning saga: `tenant.cancelled` → drop schema, revoke keys, cancel subscription, then `DELETED`. Allowed from `ACTIVE` and `FAILED`. |
| T5 | **No idempotency on `POST /tenants`.** A signup flow that retries on a timeout creates two tenants (or gets a 409 on the subdomain and shows the customer an error). | Every API customer hits this in the first week. | `Idempotency-Key` header, stored per organisation for 24 h, replaying the original response. |
| T6 | **Subdomain and name are unique across the whole platform.** Two companies can't both have a customer called "Acme". | Breaks the moment there are two organisations. | Uniqueness scoped to the organisation: `(organisationId, subdomain)` and `(organisationId, name)`. The address becomes `<subdomain>.<company base domain>`. |

### Safety and abuse

| # | Problem | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **No quotas.** Signup is open and every tenant creates a Postgres schema on a 1-vCPU box. | One script can fill the disk. | Per-organisation limits: 10 active tenants and 50 provisions a day on the free tier. Return 429 with a clear message. |
| S2 | **No email verification** and **no password reset.** Resend is live, so both are cheap now. | Unverified accounts can't be trusted with API keys; a forgotten password is a lost account. | Verify before the first API key can be created. Add forgot/reset password. |
| S3 | **The saga never times out per step**, only the 30-minute sweeper. | A stuck step looks "in flight" for half an hour. | Per-step timeout (2 min) that fails the step with a reason, so the webhook and the dashboard say so quickly. |
| S4 | **No terms or privacy pages.** Real people have already signed up. | Needed before any GTM, and the Google OAuth app will want them too. | `/terms` and `/privacy`, linked from the footer and the signup form. |

### Usability

| # | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | The dashboard was built for engineers (routing keys, exchanges, dead letters on the first screen). | **In flight:** the Simple view becomes the default; the engineering UI becomes "Under the hood". |
| U2 | A company has no way to try it without touching its real flow. | A **test mode** flag on tenants (`test: true`): provisions as normal, never bills, shown with a badge, excluded from quotas, deletable instantly. |
| U3 | A new company doesn't know what to do first. | An onboarding checklist on the Simple home until done: verify email → set base domain → create an API key → add a webhook → provision a test customer. |
| U4 | Failures are only replayable from the server with `rabbitmqadmin`. | A **Retry** button on a failed tenant (already partly there) that re-publishes the failed step, and a "Needs attention" filter. |
| U5 | The company's customer has nothing to look at while waiting. | A public, tokenised status page per tenant (`/status/<token>`) the company can link to or embed: "Setting up your workspace… 3 of 5 done". |

### Go-to-market

| # | Drawback | Response |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | **The landing page sells the architecture, not the outcome.** Founders don't buy sagas. | Rewrite the hero around the outcome ("Onboard B2B customers in seconds"), the five things they get, a 30-second demo, pricing, and a "Built on" section for the engineering story. |
| G2 | **The built-in steps live in liftoff's infrastructure.** No real SaaS wants its customers' databases in our Postgres. | Position liftoff as the *control plane* and make steps pluggable: each step is built-in, skipped, or a **custom step** that calls the company's URL and waits for a callback. That's the sellable version. Biggest item on the roadmap. |
| G3 | **No pricing.** | Free: 10 active tenants, test mode, community support. Pro: per active tenant per month, custom steps, webhooks, Stripe. Decide numbers later; the page needs the shape. |
| G4 | **No docs.** The API is Swagger only. | A `/docs` section: quickstart (key → `curl` → webhook), the five steps, webhooks and signatures, test mode, errors. |
| G5 | **Whose plan is it?** Tenants carry `starter/pro/enterprise`, which is the *customer's* plan on the company's product, but it reads like liftoff's pricing. | Rename in the UI to "customer plan", and let the organisation define its own plan names and Stripe prices. |

## 3. Roadmap

Work is grouped so that parallel sessions never edit the same service at the same time. Each phase ends deployed on the VPS with a smoke test.

**Phase 0, in flight**
- Simple view as the default, engineering UI as "Under the hood" (frontend).
- Organisations on signup, company API keys, ready webhook (tenant-service, gateway).

**Phase 1, can start now (new files only)**
- Landing page rewrite for the outcome, pricing shape, `/docs` quickstart, `/terms`, `/privacy` (G1, G3, G4, S4).

**Phase 2, after Phase 0 merges (tenant-service, gateway)**
- Idempotency keys (T5), org-scoped uniqueness and base domain (T6), quotas (S1), email verification and password reset (S2), per-step timeout (S3), test mode (U2).

**Phase 3, after Phase 2 (worker services + tenant-service events)**
- Honest billing with `SKIPPED` and per-org Stripe (T1, T2), notification to the company (T3), deprovisioning saga (T4), Retry from the UI (U4).

**Phase 4, after Phase 3 (frontend)**
- Onboarding checklist (U3), public status page (U5), customer-plan naming (G5).

**Phase 5, the product bet**
- Custom steps: built-in / skipped / webhook-with-callback per step, per organisation (G2).

## 4. What we're not doing

- Multi-region or per-tenant databases. Schema-per-tenant in one Postgres is fine for the hosted tier; custom steps cover everyone else.
- Teams, roles and invites inside an organisation. Worth doing, but after the roadmap above; one user per company is enough to sell the first version.
- Real DNS. The DNS step stays a record in our table until a company brings its own domain through a custom step.
